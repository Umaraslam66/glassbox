**Step 1 — the dice roll.** You define, say, 8 trait dimensions, each on a scale from roughly −2 to +2. Your script rolls Anna's true values: institution-trust **+1.4** (very trusting), risk appetite **−0.9** (cautious), environmentalism **+0.6**, price sensitivity **+1.1**, and so on. This vector is θ. It goes into a locked file. Nothing downstream ever reads it except the grader. This is the answer key to the exam your system is about to take.

**Step 2 — the card.** You feed θ to Gemma with a prompt like: "Write a realistic persona for a person with these trait levels. Show the traits through her life, not as labels." Out comes: *Anna, 43, school administrator in a mid-size city, votes reliably, keeps her savings in a bank account because funds feel gambly, recycles diligently, compares grocery prices…* Notice the card never says "trust = 1.4" — the trait is baked into biography. You also give her a noise instruction: "you're a real person; on questions you're lukewarm about, your answer can wobble." The card is also locked away.

**Step 3 — the interview.** Now the wall goes up. Your interviewer agent talks to Anna (Gemma role-playing the card) and sees *only text*. Watch what your trait-estimator believes as the conversation runs:

Question 1: "Tell me about a time a public service let you down." Anna: "Honestly? Rarely. When my street flooded the municipality fixed it in two days — people complain too much." After this one answer, your estimator reads the transcript and outputs: institution-trust ≈ **+1.0, blur ±0.7**. Right direction, still fuzzy. Risk appetite so far: **0.0 ± 1.0** — total ignorance, because nothing touched it yet.

Question 4: "If you got a €5,000 bonus, what would you do with it?" Anna: "Savings account. My sister does stocks, that's not for me." Estimator update: risk ≈ **−0.8 ± 0.5**. The blur on that dimension just collapsed because this question was *loaded* on exactly that trait.

Question 12: institution-trust is now **+1.3 ± 0.2**, risk **−0.9 ± 0.3**. Compare with the locked file: truth was +1.4 and −0.9. Your reader is recovering the planted person from words alone. That comparison — estimate vs. locked truth, and whether the blur was honest about its own error — *is* the Stage-2/3 validation. No humans needed.

**Step 4 — the prediction test.** Now a question Anna was never asked: "Would you support a congestion charge in your city?" Your question-encoder reads the text and profiles it: loads positively on institution-trust and environmentalism, negatively on price sensitivity. Combine with Anna's estimated traits: trust pushes yes, environmentalism pushes yes, price sensitivity pushes no → net prediction **"yes, 71%."** Then the grader goes behind the wall and asks *the real Anna* that question 50 times at temperature. She says yes 36/50 = 72%. Your 71% wasn't just the right answer — it was the right *probability*. Do this over thousands of persona×question cells and you get a calibration report card. That's the payoff no human study could even give you, since a human answers once, not 50 times.

**Step 5 — where RL happens.** The interviewer's job is to shrink blur fast. So after every question, compute reward = (total blur before) − (total blur after) − small cost per question. Question 4 above earned a big reward (risk blur went 1.0 → 0.5). A dud like "what's your favorite color?" earns ≈ 0, and pays the question cost, so net negative. Run thousands of interviews, reinforce the question choices that earned reward, and the interviewer *learns* strategy: open broad, then strike at whichever trait is still blurriest — exactly how the GRE picks its next question, except your interviewer learned it rather than following a hand-coded rule. Your headline number at the end: "trained interviewer reaches ±0.3 blur on all traits in 11 questions; random question order needs 30."

That's one full lap. The elegant part is that every lap generates its own grades, because you planted the truth yourself in Step 1.
